# Research desk — "How to Stop Procrastination" (sourced facts for the 6-part series)

Rule: every number shown on screen must exist here with a source.

## Definitions & scale

1. **Definition (procrastination)**: "voluntarily delaying an intended course of action despite expecting to be worse off for the delay." — Piers Steel, "The Nature of Procrastination: A Meta-Analytic and Theoretical Review of Quintessential Self-Regulatory Failure", *Psychological Bulletin* 133(1), 2007.
2. **80–95% of college students procrastinate** on academic tasks (engaging in the behaviour); "roughly 80–95% of college students engage in procrastination". — Steel 2007, meta-analysis of 691 sources.
3. **~15–20% of adults are chronic procrastinators** (across all domains, not just school). — Steel 2007; also Joseph Ferrari, *Still Procrastinating?* (2010), based on epidemiological samples.
4. **Classic framing**: procrastination is best understood as **self-regulation failure**, close cousin of the "primacy of short-term mood repair". — Steel 2007; Sirois & Pychyl 2013.

## Mechanism (why the brain does it)

5. **Short-term mood repair**: people procrastinate to avoid negative emotions attached to a task (boredom, anxiety, frustration, resentment, insecurity); the avoidance is *prioritized over the long-term goal*. — Sirois, F. & Pychyl, T., "Procrastination and the Priority of Short-Term Mood Regulation", *Social and Personality Psychology Compass* 7(4), 2013.
6. **"Give in to feel good"**: Pychyl's lab shorthand — avoidance buys instant relief; the relief negatively reinforces the habit. — Pychyl, T., *Solving the Procrastination Puzzle* (2013); interviews/lectures (CC-available talks).
7. **Limbic vs prefrontal**: the limbic system (fast, emotional, present-biased) competes with the prefrontal cortex (planning, abstract future); present-bias wins under stress/fatigue. — Steel 2007 (Temporal Motivation Theory builds on this dual-system view); McClure et al., *Science* 306 (2004) neural correlates of intertemporal choice.
8. **Temporal discounting / hyperbolic**: humans discount future rewards hyperbolically; "future you" feels like a stranger. — Steel 2007 (TMT: Motivation = (Expectancy × Value) / (1 + Impulsiveness × Delay)); Ainslie, G., *Picoeconomics* (1992).
9. **Affect heuristic of deadlines**: without a proximate deadline, delay cost stays near zero → infinite postponement is rational *locally*. — Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002 (below) discuss self-imposed vs imposed deadlines.

## Consequences (the price)

10. **Procrastinators end up worse off**: meta-analysis shows procrastination correlates with lower GPAs, lower salaries, shorter employment tenure; the behaviour persists despite "expecting to be worse off". — Steel 2007.
11. **Deadlines & performance**: in a proofreading-course experiment, students with **externally imposed weekly deadlines** outperformed self-imposed or end-loaded ones (≈ fewer errors, ~£/grade effect); self-imposed deadlines helped vs none but were set poorly. — Ariely, D. & Wertenbroch, K., "Procrastination, Deadlines, and Performance: Self-Control by Precommitment", *Psychological Science* 13(3), 2002.
12. **Health**: trait procrastination is associated with **higher perceived stress, more unhealthy behaviours, delayed treatment-seeking**; Sirois found procrastination linked to **cardiovascular disease and hypertension** in an older sample. — Sirois, F., "Procrastination and Stress: Exploring the Role of Self-compassion", *Self and Identity* 13(2), 2014; Sirois et al. 2015 hypertension analysis (older adults, n≈800, Health Psychology/Aging papers).
13. **Interruption cost**: office workers compensate for interruption by working **faster** and with **more overtime and stress**; fragmented work raises frustration. — Mark, G., Gudith, D., Klocke, U., "The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress", *CHI 2008*.
14. **Smartphone presence**: the mere presence of your own phone (even off, face-down) **reduces available working-memory capacity** ("brain drain"). — Ward, A. et al., "Brain Drain: The Mere Presence of One's Own Smartphone Reduces Available Cognitive Capacity", *Journal of the Association for Consumer Research* 2(2), 2017.
15. **Guilt spiral**: guilt/shame about procrastinating makes the task feel even more aversive → more avoidance; forgiveness breaks it (see #20). — Sirois & Pychyl 2013; Wohl et al. 2010.

## Tactics (what actually works)

16. **Zeigarnik effect**: unfinished/interrupted tasks are remembered ≈ 2× better than finished ones and create intrusive tension until started; starting creates cognitive pull. — Zeigarnik, B., "Das Behalten erledigter und unerledigter Handlungen", *Psychologische Forschung* 9, 1927 (replicated: Seifert & Patalano 1991, Baumeister & Masicampo 2011 show the tension is quieted by *planning*, not only finishing).
17. **2-Minute Rule**: scale any habit down to a two-minute starter version ("read one page", "open the doc"); mastery of the *art of showing up* precedes optimization. — James Clear, *Atomic Habits* (2018), ch. 13.
18. **Next physical action**: vague tasks stall; define the next *concrete, physical* action ("email Mike re: slides by 3pm"). — David Allen, *Getting Things Done* (2001).
19. **Implementation intentions**: "if situation X, then I will do Y" — meta-analysis of **94 studies, ~8,461 participants, effect size d ≈ 0.65** on goal attainment. — Gollwitzer, P. & Sheeran, P., "Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement: A Meta-analysis of Effects and Processes", *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38, 2006.
20. **Self-forgiveness reduces next-exam procrastination**: students who forgave themselves for procrastinating on midterm-1 procrastinated **less** on midterm-2. — Wohl, M., Pychyl, T., Bennett, S., "I forgive myself, now I can study: How self-forgiveness for procrastinating can reduce future procrastination", *Personality and Individual Differences* 48, 2010.
21. **Temptation bundling**: binding an instant-reward activity (audiobooks) to a dreaded one (gym) increased gym attendance (boundary conditions: only when fully committed). — Milkman, K., Minson, J., Volpp, K., "Holding the Hunger Games Hostage at the Gym", *Management Science* 60(2), 2014.
22. **Pomodoro technique**: 25-minute focused intervals + 5-minute breaks; works by making "start" trivial and creating a proximate deadline every 25 min. — Francesco Cirillo, *The Pomodoro Technique* (1980s/2006 book).
23. **Fresh-start effect**: temporal landmarks (new week/month/birthday) boost aspirational goal pursuit. — Dai, H., Milkman, K., Riis, J., "The Fresh Start Effect", *Management Science* 60(10), 2014.
24. **Precommitment/deadline setting**: self-imposed binding deadlines improve performance vs none; spread them out. — Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002.
25. **Time math (illustrative, arithmetic)**: 2 h/day of avoidance ≈ 730 h/year ≈ **30 full days/year** (730/24). Compound interest: $50/month invested at 22 vs 32 (7% avg) ≈ ≈2× at 67 (FV annuity: 22→~$153k vs 32→~$73k). Arithmetic illustration, marked as such on screen.
